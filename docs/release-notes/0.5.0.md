gsf 0.5.0 adds three sound drivers: `gsfrip` now rips the Dragon Ball games whose music plays on Webfoot Technologies' driver, Hardcore Pool, Garfield: The Search for Pooky and Games Explosion!, which play theirs on Frontline Studios' MOD player, and Chicken Shoot and Chicken Shoot 2, which play theirs on Frontline Studios' XM-like player. It also leaves out the MP2K songs that make no sound, and rips Aero the Acro-Bat and Hugo: The Evil Mirror without tags from tables in its code.

`gsfrip` runs on Windows 10 or later and requires no runtime DLLs. For macOS, the README explains how to build from source.

## Webfoot Technologies' driver

`gsfrip` rips the US releases of Dragon Ball Z: The Legacy of Goku (18 songs), The Legacy of Goku II (44) and Buu's Fury (53), and the cartridge with both Dragon Ball Z: Buu's Fury and Dragon Ball GT: Transformation. That cartridge contains three programs, each with a copy of the driver, and gets three sets: `Menu` (1 song), `Buu's Fury` (53) and `GT Transformation` (25). Each song of a song table is a song, and it plays at the music volume that the game starts with: 4 of 8, and 3 in GT Transformation. A song that loops gets two passes and a fade of 7 s, and a song that ends gets its end and a fade of 1 s. The game's sound effects play samples without a sequence and aren't ripped.

The sets were checked in mGBA 0.11. Each song plays the same trimmed and untrimmed. The sets weren't checked on the console itself.

## Frontline Studios' MOD player

`gsfrip` rips Hardcore Pool (the European release, 11 songs), Garfield: The Search for Pooky (18) and Games Explosion! (15, both US releases). Revision 2 of Garfield's European release rips as the US release. Each part of a module that the game starts at an order is a song, and it starts the way the game starts it, with Games Explosion!'s fade-in. Games Explosion!'s songs 12 to 14 are parts of the module that the game never starts: they're titled "(Unused)" and come last. Garfield's two modules are numbered one after the other. Every song loops and gets two passes and a fade of 7 s. The sound effects play samples without a sequence and aren't ripped.

The sets were checked in mGBA 0.11. Each song plays the same trimmed and untrimmed. The sets weren't checked on the console itself.

## Frontline Studios' XM-like player

`gsfrip` rips Chicken Shoot (the European release) and Chicken Shoot 2 (the US release), five songs each: the title's music and each order list of the game's levels. Each song plays at the master volume that the game sets on a new save. Every song loops and gets two passes and a fade of 7 s. The sound effects aren't ripped.

The sets were checked in mGBA 0.11. Each song plays the same trimmed and untrimmed. The sets weren't checked on the console itself.

## Changes to existing sets

- In MP2K games, a song whose notes all play at a level of 0, such as the notes of a track at a volume of 0, makes no sound and no longer gets a mini. A song without notes already got none. 18 songs of seven games are left out, among them six of Mother 3 and song 50 of Pokémon Mystery Dungeon: Red Rescue Team. The minis after such a song move down a number.
- The sets of Aero the Acro-Bat and Hugo: The Evil Mirror no longer have titles, an artist, a comment, a year or a copyright that `gsfrip` wrote from tables in its code. The minis are named and titled by song number and listed by number, such as `02 Song 1.minigsf`: the sets no longer follow the order in which a player first hears the songs. `--game` defaults to the image's file name. `--game`, `--artist`, `--year` and `--copyright` set those tags.
- A song that loops gets its two passes to the millisecond, a fade of 7 s in place of 10 s, and two new tags: `loop_start`, the time to the start of its loop, and `loop_length`, the time of one pass. A player that reads them can play another number of passes. Other players show them as metadata. A song that ends keeps its length and its fade of 1 s.
- `gsfrip` prints the warnings of a game's driver on every rip. Before, only `--list` printed them, with the rest of what it found.

The sets were tested in mGBA 0.11. The trimmed sets of the seven games with songs left out were compared with untrimmed ones. The sets weren't tested on the console.

## Other changes

- `gsfrip` uses more code from the libraries that it builds on, in place of its copies. Its sets and its messages stay the same.
- The README says how `gsfrip` finds a song's length: from the song's commands, not from a run of the console's model.

## Known limitations

- Mario Tennis: Power Tour has Camelot's version of MP2K, and `gsfrip` doesn't rip it yet. `gsfrip` names the reason.
- Revisions 0 and 1 of Garfield: The Search for Pooky's European release and the French release of Hardcore Pool aren't ripped. `gsfrip` names the reason.
- The US release of Chicken Shoot and the European release of Chicken Shoot 2 aren't ripped. `gsfrip` names the reason.
- Aero the Acro-Bat's sets play louder in foo_input_gsf 3.0.23 than in mGBA 0.11. The game switches the sound off and on after it sets up its driver, and the stub does the same. mGBA then halves the Direct Sound channels' volume, and foo_input_gsf doesn't. Each player plays a set at the level that it plays the game at.
- GT Transformation halves its music volume on one screen. Its set plays at the volume that the game starts with.
- A song plays as its commands give it. What a game's code changes while a song plays, such as a fade or a track that it mutes, stays out.
